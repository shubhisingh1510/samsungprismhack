# Deliverable 1: Elevator pitch and India problem stats

## The 30-second pitch

> Every parental-control app does one thing: it says **no**. And every child does one thing back: they find a way around it.
>
> NEST never says no. It's an agent swarm living across the Samsung home. When a child drifts into passive scrolling, the video itself turns around and dares them: *"Bet you can't build that in real life."* The lights shift to sunrise. The TV opens a quest. An hour later, they're watching a documentary starring **themselves**.
>
> Everyone else blocks the screen. NEST turns it into a door.

**10-second version:** "Parental controls restrict. Kids resist. NEST recruits: the screen itself dares the child back into the real world, and the whole Samsung home plays along."

Note on "never says no": NEST's own suggestions never block. A rule a parent sets is still enforced. If a judge presses, say exactly that.

## The problem in India

Each figure was checked against its source on 2 October 2026.

| Stat | Source | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **2.22 hours a day** of screen time for under-fives, against a 1-hour guideline | AIIMS Raipur meta-analysis of 10 studies, Cureus 2025 | It starts before school |
| **61%** of urban parents say their 9–17-year-old spends 3+ hours a day on social media, video/OTT and gaming; 15% say more than 6 hours | LocalCircles national survey | Scale |
| **39%** of those parents report more aggression, **37%** impatience, **27%** lethargy | LocalCircles | Parents see harm |
| **23.8%** of children use a smartphone in bed before sleep; **over 37%** report reduced concentration | NCPCR study, 5,811 respondents, 60 schools, 6 states | Sleep and attention |
| **37.8%** of 10-year-olds have a Facebook account and **24.3%** have Instagram, despite the age rules | NCPCR | Blocking already fails |
| Myopia in urban 5–15-year-olds rose from **4.44% (1999) to 21.15% (2019)**, projected at **48% by 2050** | Systematic review, 28,600 children, *Ophthalmic & Physiological Optics* 2021 | Physical cost |
| **About 90%** of rural 14–18-year-olds have a smartphone at home and can use it; **90.5%** used social media that week; only about half know the safety settings | ASER 2023 "Beyond Basics", 34,745 youth | Not just urban |
| The **Economic Survey 2025-26** names digital addiction a threat to the demographic dividend and suggests age-based limits | Government of India | Why now |

**The line that ties them together:** "India's answer so far is restriction, and 38% of 10-year-olds are already on Facebook. Restriction has lost. The question isn't how to block the screen; it's what the screen should turn into."

## Caveats judges may raise

- **LocalCircles is a parent-reported, opt-in survey.** Say "61% of surveyed urban parents", not "61% of children".
- **The 48% myopia figure is a linear projection**, and the link to screens is associative (near work, less outdoor time). Say "associated with".
- **The NCPCR data is from 2021.** Name the year if asked.
- The "15% of adolescents show technology addiction" figure from coverage of the Economic Survey is left out because it could not be traced to a primary study.
